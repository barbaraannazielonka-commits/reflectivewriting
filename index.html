01 / Discover

What is reflective writing?

Reflective writing explores what an experience means to you, why you responded as you did and how your understanding develops. A film can be that experience.

What?

Identify a meaningful moment. Give only enough context for the reader.

So what?

Explore why it mattered, your reactions, alternative perspectives and assumptions.

Now what?

Explain what you have learned or what you might think or do differently.

Move beyond the plot

Description: A character refuses to help a friend.
Reflection: I initially judged the character harshly, but the scene made me question how fear can influence our decisions.
02 / Features

Six features of effective reflection

01 · Personal voice

Use first person when exploring your own thinking.

02 · Specific evidence

Refer to a scene, decision, dialogue or detail.

03 · Analysis

Explain why a moment matters rather than only what happened.

04 · Multiple perspectives

Consider how others might interpret the same situation.

05 · Development

Show how your thinking changes, deepens or becomes more complex.

06 · Coherence

Connect ideas in purposeful paragraphs with appropriate transitions.

Use past tense for events and earlier reactions, present tense for current insights, and future or conditional forms for possible implications. A thoughtful, clear tone matters more than complicated vocabulary.

03 / Compare

Reflective vs. personal writing

Personal writing is a broad category that includes memories, experiences, feelings and opinions. Reflective writing can be personal, but its central purpose is to examine meaning and learning.

FeaturePersonal writingReflective writing
PurposeShare an experience or feelingExplore its significance
FocusWhat happened and how I feltWhy I reacted that way and what I learned
StructureMay follow a narrativeOften develops from experience to analysis and insight
VoiceOften first personOften first person, with analytical thinking

Personal example

I felt upset when the main character was excluded. It reminded me of a difficult experience at school.

Reflective example

I felt upset when the character was excluded. My reaction may have been influenced by my own experiences. However, the scene also made me consider how easily bystanders overlook exclusion.
04 / Vocabulary Lab

Language that reveals thinking

Explore 12 language categories. Choose expressions that genuinely match your ideas. Complete sentence starters with your own thoughts and use precise vocabulary rather than complicated words for their own sake.

From simple to sophisticated: I felt sad → I felt uneasy about the character’s decision → The decision challenged my assumptions about loyalty. Each version adds precision or depth, not just longer words.

07 / Writing Studio

Plan your own film reflection

Work independently. Do not use AI to generate, rewrite or improve your response. Your interpretation matters. You do not need to share private experiences.

Suggested structure

Introduction: introduce the film and the issue you will reflect on. Body: explore one or two significant moments, your reactions and alternative perspectives. Conclusion: explain your developed understanding. Follow your teacher’s assignment requirements.
